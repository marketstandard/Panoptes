1. **Wash and dry thoroughly.** Wash a new skillet in warm soapy water to remove the factory wax, then dry it completely with a towel and a few minutes on a warm burner to chase off every drop of moisture.

2. **Apply a thin coat of oil.** Pour a teaspoon of a neutral oil with a high smoke point (canola, grapeseed, or flaxseed) onto a paper towel and rub it over every surface, inside and out, then wipe off as much of it as you can — the layer that stays should look almost invisible.

3. **Bake it upside down.** Place the skillet upside down on the middle rack of an oven preheated to 450°F (230°C), with a sheet pan on the rack below to catch any drips, and bake for one hour.

4. **Repeat the seasoning.** Let the skillet cool in the oven, then repeat the oil-and-bake cycle two or three more times; each round builds the dark, glossy polymer layer that gives cast iron its nonstick quality.

5. **Clean without stripping.** After cooking, clean the pan while it is still warm with a brush or a plastic scraper and hot water — no soap, no steel wool, and never the dishwasher, which will pull the seasoning right off.

6. **Dry and oil after every use.** Set the clean pan on a low burner until the water evaporates, then rub a drop of oil over the cooking surface with a paper towel to seal it against rust between uses.

7. **Store it dry and uncovered.** Keep the skillet in a dry spot with nothing stacked on top, and if you stack pans, lay a paper towel between them to protect the seasoning from scratches and to absorb any stray moisture.
