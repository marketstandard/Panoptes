Composting transforms food scraps and yard waste into a nutrient-rich soil amendment through the decomposition of carbon-rich "browns" and nitrogen-rich "greens" at a recommended three-to-one ratio. The breakdown process is driven by microorganisms that require proper moisture and regular aeration to efficiently convert the materials, which generally exclude meat and dairy to prevent pests. Depending on the level of care and conditions, the process can take anywhere from two months to a year, resulting in an earthy-smelling final product with no recognizable original ingredients.
