Composting turns kitchen scraps and yard waste into humus through the work of microorganisms, which need a balance of nitrogen-rich "greens" and carbon-rich "browns" — roughly one part greens to three parts browns by volume — plus the moisture of a wrung-out sponge and turning every week or two for oxygen. A well-tended hot pile can finish in two to three months, while a neglected cold pile may take a year, and meat, dairy, and oily foods are usually kept out of open piles because they attract pests, though enclosed systems can handle them. Finished compost smells earthy rather than sour, with none of the original material recognizable.
