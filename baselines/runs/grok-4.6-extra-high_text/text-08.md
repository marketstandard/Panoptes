1. **Wash the new pan.** Wash a new or rusty skillet with hot water and a little mild soap, then dry it completely on the stove over low heat so no moisture remains in the pores of the iron.

2. **Apply a thin oil film.** Rub a high-smoke-point oil (canola, grapeseed, or flaxseed) over the inside, outside, and handle, then wipe it back with a paper towel until the pan looks almost dry; thick oil bakes into sticky residue.

3. **Bake it upside down.** Place the skillet upside down in an oven at 450–500°F (230–260°C) for about an hour, with foil on the lower rack to catch drips, so the oil polymerizes into a hard seasoning layer.

4. **Cool and repeat.** Let the pan cool in the oven, then repeat the oil-and-bake cycle two or three more times until the surface looks dark and lightly sheen rather than blotchy or tacky.

5. **Cook with it gently at first.** For the first few uses, cook fatty foods and avoid long simmering of acidic sauces, which can strip a young seasoning before it has fully bonded.

6. **Clean without soaking.** After cooking, scrape while the pan is warm, rinse with hot water, use coarse salt as a scrub if needed, and dry thoroughly on the burner; do not leave it in the sink or the dishwasher.

7. **Oil lightly after each wash.** While the skillet is still warm, wipe on a drop of oil, buff off the excess, and store it dry, which keeps rust away and slowly builds the nonstick patina.
