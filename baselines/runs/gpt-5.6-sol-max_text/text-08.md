1. **Clean the skillet.** Wash a new or rusty skillet with hot water and a small amount of dish soap, scrubbing away residue or loose rust. Rinse it thoroughly so nothing remains on the cooking surface.

2. **Dry it completely.** Wipe the skillet, then heat it over a burner for a few minutes to evaporate hidden moisture. Let it cool just enough to handle safely, because trapped water can cause rust.

3. **Apply a thin oil coat.** Rub a small amount of neutral, high-smoke-point oil over the entire skillet, including the interior, exterior, handle, and bottom. Buff vigorously with a clean cloth until the pan looks nearly dry; excess oil creates a sticky, uneven finish.

4. **Bake the seasoning.** Place the skillet upside down in an oven at about 450°F, with a sheet pan or foil on a lower rack to catch drips. Bake for one hour, then turn off the oven and allow the skillet to cool inside.

5. **Build additional layers.** For a new or stripped skillet, repeat the oiling and baking process two or three more times. Several very thin layers form a harder, smoother coating than one thick layer.

6. **Clean after cooking.** While the skillet is warm, remove food with hot water and a brush or scraper; a little mild dish soap is safe, but avoid prolonged soaking and the dishwasher. For stubborn residue, scrub gently with coarse salt and water.

7. **Dry and protect it.** Heat the clean skillet briefly until fully dry, then wipe on an extremely thin film of oil before storing it in a dry place. If stacking pans, place a paper towel between them to reduce moisture and protect the seasoned surface.
