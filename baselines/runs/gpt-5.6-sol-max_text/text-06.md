Composting converts kitchen scraps and yard waste into humus by balancing nitrogen-rich greens with carbon-rich browns, commonly at about a one-to-three ratio by volume. Microorganisms need moisture and oxygen, so the pile should be kept like a wrung-out sponge and turned every week or two, allowing a hot pile to finish much faster than a neglected cold one. Open piles generally exclude meat, dairy, and oily foods, and finished compost is earthy-smelling with no recognizable original material.
