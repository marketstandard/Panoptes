At 2:17 in the morning, Mara Venn saw a light where no light had any right to be.

She stood inside the lantern room of Cape Orison, one hand on the brass rail, while rain combed the glass in silver threads. Beyond the black churn of the Oregon coast, six miles north, Widow’s Tooth rose from its island like a broken bone. Its lighthouse had been dark since 1996, when the last keeper vanished and the Coast Guard pulled the generator.

Yet its dead lens flashed once.

Mara stopped breathing.

Two more flashes followed, quick and white. Then darkness held for five seconds. A long beam swept the rain, vanished, and returned.

Three short. Two long. Three short.

She counted the sequence again, tapping it against the rail. It repeated without variation.

“That station has no power,” she said, although the storm swallowed her voice.

Her radio crackled on the desk below. Mara hurried down the spiral stairs, boots ringing on iron. She called the coastal watch, then the harbor office. Static answered both channels.

On her third attempt, another voice came through.

Not the dispatcher. Not anyone she knew.

“Orison,” it whispered. “Do you see us?”

Mara stared at the radio. Behind her, the lighthouse mechanism turned with its steady, familiar groan. Through the lower window, Widow’s Tooth blinked again.

Three short. Two long. Three short.

She reached for the transmit button.

Then she remembered the logbook locked in her desk—the one kept by her grandfather on the night the neighboring keeper disappeared.
