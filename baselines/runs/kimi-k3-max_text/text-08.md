1. **Wash and dry the pan.** Scrub the skillet with warm, soapy water to remove any factory residue — this is the one time soap is acceptable — then dry it completely, since lingering water causes rust.

2. **Preheat the oven.** Set it to 450–500°F (230–260°C) with a rack in the middle position. A hot oven is what polymerizes the oil into a hard, slick coating.

3. **Apply a thin layer of oil.** Rub a teaspoon of high-smoke-point oil — grapeseed, flaxseed, or plain vegetable oil — over every surface of the pan, including the handle and the bottom.

4. **Wipe it almost dry.** Buff the skillet with a clean, dry cloth until it looks matte rather than glossy. Excess oil pools and turns sticky instead of seasoning properly.

5. **Bake it upside down.** Place the pan upside down on the rack with a foil-lined sheet on the rack below to catch drips, and bake for one hour.

6. **Repeat for a durable finish.** Let the pan cool until safe to handle, then repeat the oil-and-bake cycle two or three more times to build a deep, nonstick patina.

7. **Maintain it after every use.** Clean with hot water and a brush or scraper, dry the pan on the stovetop over low heat, and wipe on a whisper of oil before storing it.
