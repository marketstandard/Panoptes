Composting combines nitrogen-rich "greens" like vegetable peels and grass with carbon-rich "browns" like dried leaves and cardboard, ideally at roughly three parts browns to one part greens by volume. Microorganisms need moisture and oxygen to break the pile down, so it should feel like a wrung-out sponge and be turned every week or two, finishing in two to three months when conditions are hot or up to a year when neglected. Meat, dairy, and oily foods are usually left out of open piles because they attract pests, and finished compost smells earthy with no recognizable original material left.
