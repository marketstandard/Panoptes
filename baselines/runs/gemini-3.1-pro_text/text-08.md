1. **Scrub the pan** 
Wash the cast-iron skillet thoroughly with warm water, a little bit of mild dish soap, and a stiff brush or sponge. This removes any factory residues or old, flaky seasoning to give you a clean surface to work with.

2. **Dry it completely** 
Immediately dry the skillet with a clean towel to prevent rust. To be absolutely sure no moisture remains, place the pan on the stove over medium-low heat for a few minutes until all the water evaporates.

3. **Apply a thin coat of oil** 
Once the pan has cooled slightly but is still warm, rub a very thin layer of neutral cooking oil, like canola or vegetable oil, all over the inside and outside of the skillet. Wipe away any excess oil with a paper towel so the surface looks nearly dry, rather than greasy.

4. **Bake the skillet** 
Place the oiled skillet upside down on the center rack of your oven and bake it at 400°F (200°C) for one hour. You may want to place a sheet of aluminum foil on the bottom rack to catch any potential oil drips.

5. **Let it cool in the oven** 
After an hour, turn the oven off and leave the skillet inside until it cools down completely. This gradual cooling helps the oil polymerize and bond to the iron, creating a tough, non-stick surface.

6. **Clean gently after use** 
For daily care, clean the pan after cooking using warm water and a brush or a dedicated cast-iron scraper, avoiding harsh soaps or steel wool. If food is stuck, simmer a little water in the pan for a few minutes to loosen the debris before scrubbing.

7. **Re-oil before storing** 
After every cleaning, dry the skillet thoroughly on the stove over low heat. Once dry, wipe a tiny drop of oil over the interior cooking surface with a paper towel before putting it away to protect it from moisture and maintain the seasoning.
