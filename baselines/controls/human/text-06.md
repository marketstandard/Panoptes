Composting turns kitchen scraps and yard waste into humus, a dark, crumbly soil amendment, through the work of microorganisms that need moisture and oxygen. A good pile balances nitrogen-rich "greens" like vegetable peels with carbon-rich "browns" like dried leaves — roughly three parts browns to one part greens — and should feel like a wrung-out sponge and get turned every week or two. A hot pile can finish in two to three months while a neglected cold pile may take a year, and meat, dairy, and oily foods are usually kept out of open piles because they attract pests.
