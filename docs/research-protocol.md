# Panoptes research protocol v1

Registered 2026-08-13. Machine-readable copy: [`bench/protocol.json`](../bench/protocol.json). This document freezes the scientific questions, task definitions, split rules, metrics, and power thresholds **before further outcome tuning**.

## Thesis

AI-text attribution is a measurement problem. The evidential meaning of a detector score depends on the generating population, prevalence, domain, manipulation history, provenance, and calibration cohort. Panoptes measures that evidential strength and its failure modes.

Do not sell the work as “Panoptes is a highly accurate AI detector.” Sell it as a reproducible framework for calibrated, uncertainty-aware AI-text attribution that explicitly measures distribution shift and the transportability of detection evidence.

## Research questions

| ID | Name | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RQ1 | Calibration | When Panoptes reports a probability of AI participation, does that probability correspond to empirical frequency under a declared population? |
| RQ2 | Distribution shift | How much does attribution degrade when domain, generator, prompt, time period, or editing process changes? |
| RQ3 | Evidence aggregation | Do calibration, Bayesian aggregation, uncertainty, and abstention improve trustworthiness over raw classifier scores? |
| RQ4 | Reproducibility | Can the claim be independently verified from committed datasets, artifacts, manifests, and results? |

The strongest existing result is not the near-perfect in-domain Defactify score. It is that performance and calibration change dramatically when the evidence distribution changes.

## Tasks (not interchangeable)

| Task | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Human-only | No AI generation or substantive AI rewriting. |
| AI-generated | The majority or entirety of the content was generated by an AI system. |
| AI-assisted | A human authored the content but AI substantially generated or modified portions. |
| Unknown | Available evidence is insufficient for a supported attribution. |

Three outputs stay conceptually distinct:

1. **P(AI participation)** — generated or substantially modified.
2. **P(AI generation)** — majority or entirety generated by an AI system.
3. **Source-family attribution** — conditional similarity among supported families, plus unknown.

Watermark detection is a separate subsystem and must not inflate the passive-attribution claim.

## Split rules

Hard rule: **train → calibration → test**. Never fit or tune the calibration layer on the final test set.

- Splits are disjoint on prompt/group.
- When `author` or `source`/`story` fields exist, those groups are also disjoint.
- Default for `n_groups < 30`: nested grouped cross-validation (outer test groups untouched; inner split separates train from calibration).
- Otherwise: 60% / 20% / 20% group holdout.
- Official publisher splits are audited for leakage and are not used when they leak groups.
- Unknown-generator experiments train on known families and test held-out families as open-set rejection.

## Evidence pipeline

```text
raw score → probability calibration → LR calibration
         → evidence model → declared prior → posterior
         → uncertainty → decide or abstain
```

The likelihood ratio is

\[
\mathrm{LR}(x)=\frac{p(x\mid\mathrm{AI})}{p(x\mid\mathrm{Human})}
=\frac{p}{1-p}\cdot\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}
\]

where \(p\) is the **calibrated** cohort-conditional probability and \(\pi\) is the calibration-cohort prevalence. Calibration happens **before** the posterior is reported. Expected calibration error (ECE) is a diagnostic of that map; it does not discount or rescale the posterior.

Evidence dependence is not assumed from a non-rejected Durbin–Watson test. Compare:

- **naive_sum** — sum of segment log-likelihood ratios;
- **correlated_shrinkage** — shrink the sum by effective sample size under estimated within-document correlation;
- **document_level** — one evidence estimate from the whole document.

## Primary metrics

| Family | Metrics |
| --- | --- |
| Ranking | AUROC, AUPRC |
| Low-FPR | TPR @ 0.1%, 1%, 5% FPR |
| Probability quality | Brier, ECE, calibration slope/intercept |
| Selective prediction | selective risk at coverage 100%, 95%, 90%, 80%, 70%, 50% |
| Robustness | worst-group, train-domain × test-domain transport matrix |
| Attribution | macro-F1, unknown-rejection AUROC |

Accuracy at 0.5 is not a primary scientific metric.

## Priors, coverage, mixtures

- Prevalence grid for posterior sensitivity: 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%.
- The same LR produces different posteriors under different real-world prevalence assumptions.
- Mixture rates for coauthoring pilots: 0%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, 100% AI contribution.
- The scientific test is whether estimated AI participation tracks controlled participation.

## Provenance levels

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| P0 | No provenance. |
| P1 | Self-declared model identity. |
| P2 | Authenticated provider metadata. |
| P3 | Cryptographically signed generation receipt (verified C2PA). |
| P4 | Independently verifiable execution (hash-pinned run, Merkle root, optional OpenTimestamps). |

A model’s self-reported identity is not independently verified provenance.

## Power

The existing two-proportion gate (n ≈ 3,140 to detect a five-point accuracy gain at α = 0.05 with 80% power) remains the admission rule for neural-tier comparisons. Calibration, transport, and abstention claims use the same group-disjoint protocol even when that gate fails.

## What not to claim

- That AI text is universally identifiable.
- That ECE discounts a posterior.
- That failing to reject segment autocorrelation proves independence.
- That source-family similarity is exact model identity.
- That a negative watermark test is evidence of human authorship.

The scientifically stronger question is: **under what conditions is AI participation statistically identifiable, and how does the evidence degrade when those conditions change?**
